## Assets and boundaries

- **Condition worksheet:** planned `/guides/condition/checklist` on https://cambridgetcg.com — printable manual inspection notes and separate horizontal/vertical border ratios. No image upload, automatic condition tier, grade, authenticity or value prediction. [PSA grading standards](https://www.psacard.com/gradingstandards) provide external grading context, not certification of our tool; direct fetching returned 403, so no numerical PSA claims are reproduced here.
- **Identifier reference:** planned `/standards/validator` — existing CambridgeTCG SKU syntax validation, explicit normalization suggestions and builder. Valid syntax does not establish a real card, printing or catalog match. It neither replaces other projects’ IDs nor claims adoption of our standard.
- Keep both public references non-commercial: no paid links, reciprocal bargains, registration funnel or new tracking. Existing private pricing stays behind login; admission remains paused. `/pulls`, `/artists` and `/guides/from-japan` are supplementary, not substitutes for these flagships.

## Five verified public prospects

Each channel below was readable without authentication. General feedback channels are identified honestly; none is represented as an invitation to publish our link. Notes use natural resource names, with no prescribed anchor text.

### 1. The Cardboard Connection

**Evidence/fit:** [Comprehensive Guide to Card Grading](https://www.cardboardconnection.com/comprehensive-guide-card-grading) discusses condition, centering and inspection; its [resource directory](https://www.cardboardconnection.com/collectopedia/resources) links collecting guides. A printable observations-only companion could serve those readers.

**Published channel:** [Contact form](https://www.cardboardconnection.com/contact-us), offered for feedback to editorial staff; not a dedicated resource-submission queue. Its exclusions include grading and appraisal requests.

**Unsent; asset not deployed:** “Your grading guide explains condition and centering. We’re preparing a free CambridgeTCG inspection worksheet that records observations and border ratios without assigning a grade. Would that help readers preparing their own notes? An optional reference is entirely your editorial choice. Please correct any misleading inspection language.”

### 2. Sleeve No Card Behind

**Evidence/fit:** [Write for Us](https://sleevenocardbehind.com/write-for-us/) identifies card care, handling, storage and collector education as its remit. The worksheet could turn that practical advice into a printable record.

**Published channel:** [Contact page](https://sleevenocardbehind.com/contact/) publishes the organizational inbox `sleevenocardbehind@gmail.com` and expressly welcomes corrections. The writing page links this contact route but specifies no submission workflow.

**Unsent; asset not deployed:** “Your card-care focus seems a good place to ask about our planned free inspection worksheet. It records surfaces, edges, corners and separate border ratios, not grades. Would it complement your educational material? Refer to it only if useful; please flag missing checks or wording that could encourage overconfidence.”

### 3. Cardhound Vintage

**Evidence/fit:** [Understanding Qualifiers at PSA](https://cardhoundvintage.com/vintage-faq-understanding-qualifiers-at-psa/) discusses off-centering and other defects. Relevance is an editorial hypothesis: recording defects separately could support that discussion without predicting qualifiers.

**Published channel:** [Contact form](https://cardhoundvintage.com/contact/), “Questions/Comments”; also organizational `info@cardhoundvintage.com`. General feedback, not a dedicated editorial pitch desk.

**Unsent; asset not deployed:** “Your qualifier explainer distinguishes off-centering from other condition problems. Our planned printable inspection worksheet keeps those observations separate and predicts no grade or qualifier. Would that be useful alongside your explanation? Any reference is optional. Please correct anything that fails for vintage or unusually cut cards.”

### 4. Draftsim

**Evidence/fit:** [Valuing Magic cards](https://draftsim.com/mtg-cards-value/) has a ‘Determine the Condition’ section covering whitening, creases, warping and damage. The worksheet fits physical inspection, not Draftsim’s pricing conclusions.

**Published channel:** [Contact form](https://draftsim.com/contact/) accepts site-improvement suggestions, developer contributions and bug reports. No separate editorial inbox verified.

**Unsent; asset not deployed:** “Your condition section names damage readers can inspect themselves. We’re preparing a free printable worksheet for recording those observations and border ratios, with no valuation or grading output. Would it help that section’s readers? Refer to it only if it earns its place; corrections to Magic-specific inspection assumptions are welcome.”

### 5. PkmnCards

**Evidence/fit:** [PkmnCards](https://pkmncards.com/) provides card images and illustrator browsing. The proposed fit is explaining a limit: a reference image identifies an illustration, not a collector’s physical copy’s condition. Its remit is primarily a database, so priority is lower.

**Published channel:** [Site-owner contact form](https://pkmncards.com/contact/), a general message route; no resource-list submission policy verified. No personal email retained.

**Unsent; asset not deployed:** “Your reference images help collectors locate a card, but cannot show wear on their own copy. Our planned manual inspection worksheet makes that distinction explicit and offers no image-based grade. Would that clarification be useful to readers? Any reference is optional; please correct assumptions about your image resource.”
