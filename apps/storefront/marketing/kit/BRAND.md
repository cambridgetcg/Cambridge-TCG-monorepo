# Cambridge TCG: brand kit

> Building open-source tools and shared standards for card identity, images, prices and market data.

Generated by `artcraft-brand brandkit.py 1.5` with vectorcraft-cli 0.13.1, from `source/brand.json`. Every vector and raster was drawn by VectorCraft in one headless session (`brand.vectorcraft` is that document); stdlib Python did the colour maths, the ICO packing and these documents. Open `index.html` to review everything on light and dark grounds.

## Logo

**Mark:** from `source/mark-source.svg`, 3 shape(s) in #9B3B12, normalised by VectorCraft (transforms baked, viewBox offset removed) and rebuilt from its path data; its simplified version `source/mark-small.svg` (mark.small) draws the favicons, 48 px and under. **Wordmark:** "Cambridge TCG" in Inter SemiBold, tracking -40/1000 em, outlined. Every SVG is outlined paths with no `<text>`, no strokes and named ids.

Proportions are derived from the mark, so they hold at any size:

- **Horizontal lockup:** wordmark cap height = 0.50 x mark height; mark-to-word gap = 0.30 x mark height. The wordmark is centred on the mark by its cap height (the band from baseline to the top of a capital H), not by its bounding box, so descenders and lowercase letters do not pull it off centre.
- **Stacked lockup:** cap height = 0.32 x mark height; gap from the mark to the top of the capitals = 0.21 x mark height; both centred on one axis.

**Clear space.** Keep a margin of at least **x** on every side of any logo, where **x is the wordmark's cap height** (the height of a capital H as set in the lockup). For the horizontal lockup x = 0.50 x the mark's height (48 px at the 1x size, 96 px tall). For the mark alone, use x = a quarter of the mark's height. Nothing else (text, edges, other logos) enters that space.

**Minimum sizes** (below these the wordmark loses its letterforms; use the mark alone):

| asset | screen | print |
|---|---|---|
| wordmark | 120 px wide (cap height 12 px) | 29 mm wide (cap height 3 mm) |
| horizontal lockup | 150 px wide (cap height 12 px) | 36 mm wide (cap height 3 mm) |
| stacked lockup | 120 px wide (cap height 12 px) | 29 mm wide (cap height 3 mm) |
| mark | 16 px (below 24 px use the hinted favicon cuts) | 6 mm |

**Colour versions.** Full colour on light grounds. The `-reversed` lockups and `wordmark-reversed` are for dark grounds: the wordmark is #FFF6E9, 16.63:1 on #1D1425, and #9B3B12 becomes #FFB078, the colour brand.json chooses for dark grounds (`mark.dark`; 9.95:1 and APCA Lc -68.6 on #1D1425). On dark grounds a mark colour moves up its own scale until it reaches 3:1 and APCA Lc 30 (the dark-mode UI bar). A badge that holds letters takes that same UI step: with its letters when they keep 4.5:1 on it, else with its letters cut out when the ground shows through them at 4.5:1, else with its letters in the ink that reads on it; only when none of these works does it take the step that keeps its letters at 4.5:1. White only when no step does. A colour brand.json's `mark.dark` names is used instead of that step, and is held to the same bar (the build fails when it falls short). The one-colour marks are every shape in one ink.

**Do not:** recolour outside the palette, stretch, rotate, add effects or outlines, set the name in another font, rebuild the lockup by hand, or place the full-colour logo on a ground close to its own colours (a fill of the brand colour, a busy photo): use the one-colour or reversed versions there.

## Colour

Hex is the source value (sRGB). OKLCH is shown for reference and drives the scales; it is never written as a fill in any vector file. Contrast is WCAG 2.2 (the conformance bar); APCA Lc is in `colour/contrast.md` as guidance.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| primary | `#9B3B12` | `oklch(48.1% 0.138 40.4)` | 155 59 18 | C24 M76 Y100 K18 (out of gamut) | 6.94 | 3.03 |
| accent | `#EC7838` | `oklch(69.6% 0.162 46.7)` | 236 120 56 | C3 M60 Y78 K0 | 2.87 | 7.31 |
| neutral | `#2C1836` | `oklch(25.1% 0.060 313.5)` | 44 24 54 | C79 M93 Y42 K51 | 16.27 | 1.29 |
| light ground | `#FBF7F1` | `oklch(97.8% 0.009 78.3)` | 251 247 241 | C2 M2 Y5 K0 | 1.07 | 19.68 |
| dark ground | `#1D1425` | `oklch(21.1% 0.035 307.9)` | 29 20 37 | C74 M76 Y45 K69 | 17.81 | 1.18 |

CMYK is VectorCraft's conversion (`color.convert`) into its default CMYK working space, rounded to whole percent: a starting point for a printer, not a proof. Ask for a proof on the actual stock before a print run; spot colours are not chosen here.

Each colour has an 11-step OKLCH scale (50 to 950) in `colour/tokens.css`, `tokens.json` and the swatch libraries; the brand colour itself is the step nearest its lightness.

**Roles** (what the tokens are for). Each role is the step of its scale nearest the brand colour that reaches its WCAG 2.2 bar and an APCA floor: |Lc| 45 for text (`text-muted`, `*-text`) and 30 for UI (`*-ui`, `focus`), because WCAG 2 over-rates light colours on dark grounds. On dark grounds the logo uses #FFB078 for #9B3B12, the colour brand.json chooses there (`mark.dark`, held to the same 3:1 and APCA Lc 30 bar), while dark mode uses `--primary-ui` #CA6540 for focus rings and controls. `--surface` and `--border` are derived from the light and dark grounds you gave: the same lightness steps away from them as the neutral 100/200 (light) and 900/800 (dark) steps sit from the default grounds, in the ground's own hue, so cards and rules show on it. `on-*` is the ink on a brand fill: the brand's dark text colour or white, whichever reads better at 4.5:1, the same ink the logo uses.

**Contrast summary:** 20 of 20 required pairs pass (text 4.5:1, UI and focus 3:1, in both modes).
Brand colours as fills on the light ground (logos are exempt from WCAG; these are for judgement): primary 6.5:1, accent 2.69:1.

**In code.** `colour/tokens.css` defines `--<scale>-<step>` and the roles (`--bg`, `--surface`, `--text`, `--primary` …) for light and dark mode. For Tailwind CSS v4, `@import "./colour/tailwind.theme.css";` after `@import "tailwindcss";` gives every step as a utility (`bg-primary-600`, `text-neutral-700`) and the roles as utilities that follow the mode (`bg-bg`, `bg-surface`, `text-text-muted`, `border-border`, `ring-focus`, `bg-primary` with `text-on-primary`), plus `font-display`, `font-body` and `font-mono`. For Tailwind v3, `theme.extend.colors = require("./colour/tailwind.colors.js")` gives the same class names; load `tokens.css` too, because the roles there are `var(--…)`. The brand's neutral scale replaces Tailwind's own `neutral`.

## Typefaces and licences

Every face below was drawn by VectorCraft and outlined (or rasterised), so no file of the kit needs the font installed. The licence still governs using the face in a logo and on a website.

| face | used for | licence |
|---|---|---|

## Icons

- **App icons** (`apple-touch-icon`, `icon-192`, `icon-512`): full-bleed #9B3B12 tile, the mark in one ink, #FBF7F1 (icon.ink). The mark spans 70% of the tile (measured; icon.padding 0.18 a side), sized optically (sparse or wide marks a little larger, solid blocks a little smaller) and centred between its box centre and its centre of mass. No rounded corners are baked in: iOS and Android apply their own masks.
- **Maskable** (`icon-maskable-512`): same tile, the mark pulled in to 56% of the tile, so its farthest pixel, 175 px from the centre, sits inside the 204.8 px safe circle (the aim is about 176 px, leaving a margin).
- **Favicons:** #9B3B12 tile, 22% corner radius. Each size has its own cut:
  The cuts are drawn from the small mark, `source/mark-small.svg` (mark.small), not the full mark.

  | size | cut | mark size | margins (left, top, right, bottom), measured | clean pixels, predicted |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 16 px | the small mark | 12 px | 3, 2, 3, 2 | 80% |
  | 32 px | the small mark | 26 px | 5, 3, 5, 3 | 87% |
  | 48 px | the small mark | 38 px | 8, 5, 8, 5 | 91% |

  Each cut is hinted before it is drawn. Its box is set on whole pixels; every cut is centred to the pixel. Stems and bars that are straight, or within half a pixel of straight (a serif face's slightly flared stems, the longest straight part of a stem that flows into its bracket, and three quarters of a pixel for a long straight edge), are made straight, and their edges and every curve's outermost point move to whole pixels, shape by shape, so those strokes render as solid pixels rather than half-tones; diagonals and curves stay anti-aliased. Points two shapes share (a common vertex or edge) are welded to one position, so no crack of the ground opens where they meet (verify checks the pixels deep inside the ink for it), and where hinting closes a thin gap between two shapes of one colour, the speck it would leave at the gap's end is filled. A band that holds a stroke keeps at least one pixel however thin, so hinting never deletes an arm or a bar (verify compares every cut with its unhinted outline to prove it); a stem keeps its own width rounded, so equal stems stay equal; edges that nearly meet share a pixel line, so a counter never notches the stem beside it; a counter keeps a pixel only where it separates strokes that face each other. A shape that is mirror-symmetric about its box is hinted on one side and mirrored, and two shapes that mirror each other share one hinting, so a symmetric mark stays symmetric; when most of a mark mirrors about an axis of its own, contour by contour (a symmetric body beside an ornament off its axis), that part is hinted about its axis set on a pixel edge or centre, and the rest beside it (verify checks the pixels of both cases). On a tile, the cut is made 2 px smaller until no ink pixel touches the tile's soft rounded corner. `favicon.svg` carries the 32 and 16 px cuts and shows the 16 px one when drawn under 24 px; `favicon.ico` holds the 16, 32 and 48 px renders.

**Tabs.** The tile #9B3B12 against Chrome's tab colours: white (light, active tab) 6.94:1 (OKLab dE 0.54), #DEE1E6 (light, tab strip) 5.29:1 (OKLab dE 0.45), #202124 (dark, tab strip) 2.32:1 (OKLab dE 0.27), #35363A (dark, active tab) 1.74:1 (OKLab dE 0.20). It stands apart from every tab colour by lightness.

## Website head

Copy the files in `web/` to the site root and put this early in `<head>` (from `web/head-snippet.html`). It is the minimal modern set; obsolete tags (`shortcut icon`, `mask-icon`, `msapplication-*`, extra apple-touch sizes) are deliberately left out.

```html
<!-- Icons: three links and the manifest. Serve the files in web/ at the site root. -->
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.ico" sizes="32x32">
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.svg" type="image/svg+xml">
<link rel="apple-touch-icon" href="/apple-touch-icon.png">
<link rel="manifest" href="/site.webmanifest">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#FBF7F1" media="(prefers-color-scheme: light)">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#1D1425" media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">

<!-- Social preview: keep these early in <head>. og:title should be the page title, not the site name; pages with their own card have their lines in og/head-<slug>.html. -->
<meta property="og:type" content="website">
<meta property="og:site_name" content="Cambridge TCG">
<meta property="og:title" content="TCG data. Made to connect.">
<meta property="og:locale" content="en_GB">
<meta property="og:description" content="Building open-source tools and shared standards for card identity, images, prices and market data.">
<meta property="og:url" content="https://cambridgetcg.com">
<meta property="og:image" content="https://cambridgetcg.com/og.jpg">
<meta property="og:image:type" content="image/jpeg">
<meta property="og:image:width" content="1200">
<meta property="og:image:height" content="630">
<meta property="og:image:alt" content="Cambridge TCG card: the Cambridge TCG logo mark beside the headline “TCG data. Made to connect.” on a very light warm grey background.">
<meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image">

<!-- Google Search: the organisation's logo (at least 112 px, crawlable, right on white). -->
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
 "@context": "https://schema.org",
 "@type": "Organization",
 "name": "Cambridge TCG",
 "url": "https://cambridgetcg.com",
 "logo": "https://cambridgetcg.com/icon-512.png"
}
</script>
```

`web/site.webmanifest` sets `"display": "standalone"`, `theme_color` #FBF7F1 and `background_color` #FBF7F1 (the defaults: set "app" in brand.json for "browser", "minimal-ui" or the dark ground). Its `lang` is en-GB.

With a `url`, the snippet ends with a schema.org `Organization` block for Google Search: its logo is `https://cambridgetcg.com/icon-512.png`, the square app icon (512 px, opaque, so it looks right on white as Google asks). Keep that file at that URL; Google shows the logo only if it chooses to.

**Social card** (`web/og.jpg`): light theme, title "TCG data. Made to connect." at 68 px in 2 lines (15.24:1), the subtitle at 30 px in Light, the mark at the right, kept inside the centred 1200 x 600 band that X crops to. Every line is measured and set inside its column, clear of the mark and the footer (`og.text_fits` checks the pixels too); lines break at spaces, preferring the end of a sentence, a colon, a dash or a comma. Baseline JPEG, one interleaved scan, sRGB profile. Change the image URL when the card changes: platforms cache by URL. Its `og:image:alt` describes the picture: "Cambridge TCG card: the Cambridge TCG logo mark beside the headline “TCG data. Made to connect.” on a very light warm grey background."

Notes from the research, untested on devices: iOS 26 Safari reportedly ignores `theme-color` and tints from the page background instead (WebKit bug 301756), while Chrome on Android and installed apps still use it. A social card cannot follow light or dark mode (`og:image` has no media query); for a README or a hero image that should, use `<picture>` with `logo/lockup-horizontal.svg` and a `prefers-color-scheme: dark` source of `lockup-horizontal-reversed.svg`. Apple's Messages guidance (TN3156) says to avoid text in preview images; this card carries the title because X shows a link's headline only as a small overlay on the image, if at all (research, 2024), a trade-off to know. A `monochrome` manifest icon is optional and its support unconfirmed; `logo/mark-mono-black.svg` could serve as one.

## What this kit does not make

Recipes are in `~/.kingdom/tools/artcraft-brand/references/recipes.md`; each needs its own checks.

- Social banners and headers (X header, LinkedIn cover, YouTube channel art, Facebook cover): not covered; each platform's sizes and the area its avatar covers need checking first. FigCraft's social-posts template covers post images.
- A print guideline PDF: recipes.md §3 (DesignCraft one-pager). `index.html` prints to a usable sheet (print styles keep its dark grounds).
- Business cards with bleed and crop marks: recipes.md §4 (DesignCraft).
- CMYK separations, spot colours and press proofs: the CMYK above is an approximate conversion only.
- A BIMI logo for email inboxes: recipes.md §9 lists its prerequisites (SVG Tiny PS, a mark certificate, DMARC enforcement); not a recipe.
- Figma or FigCraft variables: recipes.md §5 (FigCraft colour variables, with guards).
- More page cards than the 40 that "pages" takes, or cards from a CMS: recipes.md §1 (DesignCraft data merge) and §2 (PhotoCraft data sets).

## Rebuild and verify

Edit `source/brand.json` and run, from inside this folder:

```sh
python3 ~/.kingdom/tools/artcraft-brand/scripts/brandkit.py source/brand.json --out . --rebuild-check
python3 ~/.kingdom/tools/artcraft-brand/scripts/verify.py .        # re-run every check; rewrites verify.json and the section below
```

The build happens in a sibling staging folder and replaces the kit's files only when it finishes, so a failed build leaves this kit as it was. It is deterministic: rebuilding gives byte-identical files, except that the two PDFs can differ, because vectorcraft-cli 0.13.1 can take their creation dates and IDs from the clock. `--rebuild-check` proves it each time by building again from `source/brand.json` in a temp folder and comparing every other file byte for byte.

## What was not checked

- Rendering in real browsers: tab favicons, the favicon.svg size switch and dark-scheme rule, and how a browser chooses between favicon.ico and favicon.svg were not observed.
- Home-screen and launcher masks on real iOS and Android devices; only the 40% safe circle was measured.
- Social previews on live platforms (X, LinkedIn, Slack, iMessage): only file size, dimensions, tags, the JPEG scan layout and a decode by djpeg or sips were checked. Post a test link once before relying on the card.
- Print: PDF colour stays RGB; the CMYK values in BRAND.md are VectorCraft's conversion, not a proof; no bleed or spot colours.
- Font licences beyond what BRAND.md "Typefaces and licences" states: faces bundled with vectorcraft-cli are OFL-1.1; the licence of any other face was not checked.
- Email clients (the on-light PNG in a signature: dark modes, scaling), platforms' avatar crops, and whether Google uses the Organization logo were not observed; the Tailwind files were checked as text against palette.json, not built with Tailwind.
- APCA contrast is guidance (WCAG 3 drafts); the colour roles use it as a floor, but WCAG 2.2 is the bar that was checked for conformance.
- The design itself: the kit reproduces the mark you supplied; it does not judge originality, trademark clearance or cultural reading.
